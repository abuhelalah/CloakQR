# Privacy Policy for CloakQR

**Last updated:** 4 August 2026

CloakQR ("the app") is a privacy-first QR code scanner and generator developed
by Abu Helalah ("we", "us"). This Privacy Policy explains how the app handles
your information. In short: **CloakQR does not collect, store, or share any
personal data, and it has no internet access.**

## Summary

- We do **not** collect any personal data.
- We do **not** use analytics, advertising, or tracking of any kind.
- The app has **no internet permission** and cannot transmit data off your
  device.
- All processing happens **locally on your device**.
- There are **no accounts** and **no sign-up**.

## Information We Collect

**None.** CloakQR does not collect, transmit, or share any personal or usage
information. We have no servers and receive nothing from the app.

## Data Stored on Your Device

Some information is saved **only on your device** so the app can work. It never
leaves your phone and is never sent to us or any third party:

- **Scan history** — QR codes you scan or create may be saved to a local
  history on your device. You can turn history off or clear it at any time in
  Settings. Wi-Fi passwords are excluded from history by default.
- **App settings** — your preferences (such as theme, text size, and language)
  are stored locally on your device.

Uninstalling the app removes this locally stored data.

## Permissions

CloakQR requests a single permission:

- **Camera** — used only to scan QR codes in real time. Camera images are
  processed on-device and are never recorded, saved, or transmitted unless you
  explicitly choose to save a code.

The app does **not** request internet, location, contacts, storage-tracking, or
any other sensitive permission beyond the camera.

## Children's Privacy

CloakQR does not collect personal data from anyone, including children. The app
is safe for users of all ages.

## Third-Party Services

CloakQR does not include any third-party analytics, advertising, or tracking
SDKs. When you choose to open a scanned link, it is handled by another app you
select (such as your browser); that app's own privacy policy then applies.
